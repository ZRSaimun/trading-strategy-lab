# Paper risk-policy tightening review (disconnected)

`backend/app/paper_risk_policy_tightening.py` provides a **pure, read-only**, fail-closed comparison of two **caller-declared** paper risk policies. The caller must supply a trusted server clock and exact account/provider/currency identity. The evaluator requires a one-step revision increase and monotonic effective timestamp, never allows the emergency kill switch to be cleared, and flags any weakening of position loss, daily loss, gross notional, margin, leverage, spread, quote freshness, order-count, cooldown or loss-streak limits. Its own policy limits bound inputs even if a caller tries to weaken both policies.

A structurally passing comparison is **not** permission to change a production policy, submit a paper order or submit a live order. Every result is `HOLD_RECONCILIATION` with `may_execute_real_order=false` and `may_execute_paper_order=false`. The SHA-256 digest is diagnostic, not a signature or owner approval. It does not authenticate policies, inventory, time or quotes; it does not persist anything, log to the database, or contact a broker.

Integration requirements: load the complete active policy and reservation inventory from an authenticated server-owned source under a transactional lock; compare revisions and the proposed policy atomically with persistent audit, open/pending/unknown-order reconciliation, daily-loss latch and owner authorization. Reject concurrent or stale revisions. A policy loosening or emergency-latch reset must require a **separate** authenticated, audited, out-of-band owner-reviewed workflow and fresh broker inventory. Do not treat any review fingerprint as execution permission. Every individual real-money order still requires positive owner confirmation and valid provider credentials.

`pytest -q backend/tests/test_paper_risk_policy_tightening.py` runs synthetic tests. No schema migration, route, paid resource, secret or execution endpoint is added. Trading 212's documented public API is Invest/Stocks ISA only, not CFD; do not infer CFD order support from this review.
