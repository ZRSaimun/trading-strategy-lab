# Loop 5: causal recency-weighted frequency baseline (offline only)

`frontend/lib/causal-ewma-frequency.ts` adds a second, explicitly uncalibrated probabilistic research baseline alongside the existing Jeffreys-smoothed unconditional frequency model. It estimates upward movement over **1, 3 and 12 completed-candle horizons** using non-overlapping h-step outcomes from a single exact source identity. Older outcomes receive exponentially decaying weight by their age in outcome samples. Default half-life is 20 outcomes, with configurable bounded windows. Effective sample size is `(sum(weights)^2)/sum(weights^2)`; the model abstains if fewer than 20 raw or 10 effective observations are available. A 0.5/0.5 shrinkage on the effective sample count avoids exact zero/one predictions. This is a heuristic frequency estimator, **not** a conjugate Bayesian posterior, calibrated probability or established trading edge.

Every candle passes the existing strict `estimateCausalFrequencyBaseline` validator first: exact feed/price side, contiguous aligned completed timestamps, positive prices, monotonic receipt times, bounded delay, and issuance before the next completed candle. All estimates use data received no later than the forecast issuance timestamp. No missing bars, future data, provider mixing or horizon overlap are silently accepted. Different horizons are reported independently. The caller's feed identity and clock remain **unauthenticated**, and the resulting forecast is `UNVERIFIED_LOCAL_RESEARCH`, `live=false`, `persisted=false`, `execution=NONE`, `quoteTimestamp=null`.

**Testing:** `cd frontend && node --experimental-strip-types --test tests/causal-ewma-frequency.test.ts`. Synthetic regression cases cover three horizons, disjoint windows, recency shifts, effective sample abstention, source contamination, gaps, timestamp causality, parameter validation, and immutability. Synthetic fixtures do not measure market accuracy, Brier skill, P&L, drawdown, or broker fills. An EWMA can perform worse than the static frequency benchmark during noisy reversals, class imbalance or regime changes. Compare both models in genuinely prospective prequential scoring before relying on either.
